Student mission and model notes

Make 20 annual policy choices. Elections are triggered after years 5, 10, 15 and 20. A public approval score below 55 may cause a one-point annual political cost for the next five-year term; severe deterioration or very low approval may cause election defeat and end your run. If an election is won, legitimacy can recover. For classroom purposes the 1-point cost is labelled political patronage / damage control; it is deliberately not counted as improved community wellbeing.

Target after Year 20: forest ≥ 60, output ≥ 85, wellbeing ≥ 65, and survival through all elections. Different strategies may achieve different outcomes. The system includes delayed environmental effects, occasional deterministic external shocks, and election rules. All equations, thresholds and indices are fictional teaching assumptions, not empirical Amazon measurements, election forecasts or claims about Brazilian governance.

The simulation represents simplified policy trade-offs; it does not represent Indigenous governance adequately, and it is not a substitute for local or scientific modelling.

Suggested discussion questions
  1. Why did increasing farm output sometimes make forest protection harder?
  2. Could an administration win an election while environmental conditions deteriorated?
  3. Did short-term political spending fix the underlying causes of dissatisfaction?
  4. Which of your decisions had the largest delayed effect five or ten years later?
  5. Is a five-year election cycle compatible with twenty-year ecological restoration?
